## Borksen-missing / soldier-friend boundary

Five of Tserriednich's soldier friends gather on Tier 3 while the special funeral security alert remains in effect. Otocin is surprised to learn that Borksen is missing. Momolly and the others establish that Borksen normally would have reported any unusual change and that her silence therefore requires concern.

The soldiers consider **abduction** the worst-case explanation, but Chapter 407 does not show them witnessing the capture or learning its mechanism. Their proposed methods remain hypotheses: a coordinated team hiding the victim in the crowd, an immobilizing drug, a familiar person luring her toward a hospital, or an unknown Nen effect comparable to hypnosis. Otocin's idea that Heil-Ly might use Borksen in an assassination attempt on Tserriednich is also speculation, not a confirmed Heil-Ly objective.

They remember their earlier dilemma about whether a captured member should trigger negotiation or rescue. A superior interrupts the gathering and orders them to maintain proper spacing because the special security alert is still active. They postpone any group decision until perimeter duty is over.

## Borksen captivity / memory-gap boundary

Borksen wakes inside the **Tier 2 Heil-Ly hideout** with Morena sitting across from her. Morena directly identifies the location and asks Borksen to become an ally. Borksen is not physically restrained, but she counts six enemies around her and finds that her weapon has been confiscated.

Borksen cannot remember how she was brought there. She infers that some power she does not understand may have been used and quickly considers Nen as a possibility. Chapter 407 therefore confirms Borksen's captivity in the Heil-Ly hideout but **does not identify the abductor, exact capture route, drug, ability, Nen user, activation condition, or method**. Earlier archive knowledge that Heil-Ly had prepared kidnapping tools or personnel is not converted into proof of who executed Borksen's capture.

## Recruitment / time-pressure boundary

Morena says she wants Borksen as an ally and compares her to a compatible donor. She says joining is not mandatory, but the group will do everything it can to persuade her. Morena also warns that the more she explains, the less room her side has to change what comes next and that a longer exchange becomes more dangerous for both sides.

These statements establish coercive time pressure and an asymmetrical negotiation environment. They do not establish the still-unsupplied substantive reason Morena wants Borksen, the exact ability Morena wants from her, or a confirmed future consequence beyond the rules Morena actually explains in Chapter 407.

## Negotiation-game core procedure

Morena proposes a negotiation game framed as a **parent** making a request to a **child** until the child is left with an answer.

- Morena, the parent, has **seven face-up cards**.
- Borksen, the child, has **five face-down response cards**.
- The child first selects one parent card; the parent answers according to that card, and the card goes to the graveyard.
- The parent then selects one child card, turns it face up, and that card goes to the graveyard.
- The sequence repeats until one child card remains; that final child card becomes the answer to the parent's request.

Morena says that if Borksen retires instead of playing, Borksen will lose every freedom except the ability to answer yes or no. Borksen agrees only to hear the full rules at that stage.

## Child-card boundary

Chapter 407 supplies all five child-side response cards:

- **Yes** — a yes answer if it becomes the final answer card; Borksen internally understands this as joining Morena and accepting whatever rules follow.
- **No** — a no answer, but Chapter 407 deliberately leaves its full downstream consequence for the parent-side No? explanation rather than resolving it.
- **R / Return** — allows one child card from the child's graveyard to return to the hand; if Return itself survives until the end, Morena says it can be exchanged for the card that produces the answer Borksen wants.
- **Joker** — can transform into either yes or no and can express Borksen's intention if it is the final card.
- **X** — neither yes nor no; if X is the final card, Borksen may leave without answering, and Morena promises that Heil-Ly will not force the negotiation on her again even if another opportunity arises.

Borksen analyzes the apparent friendliness of the response set as a possible trap. Her internal assessment that Yes would be total defeat, that No may reveal hidden costs, and that the parent could theoretically cheat are **Borksen's analysis**, not independent narrator rulings about future outcomes.

## Parent-card boundary

Chapter 407 supplies the seven parent-side cards and their Chapter 407 explanation boundary:

- **Aim** — Morena will explain the purpose of bringing Borksen there and recruiting her, with follow-up questions allowed about that purpose.
- **Power** — Morena will explain her own ability truthfully and without deception for as long as needed for Borksen to understand it.
- **Question A** — Borksen may ask as many questions as she wants outside Aim/Power scope, but Morena's answer format is limited to yes, no, or yes-and-no.
- **Question B** — on a later turn, Borksen may request a fuller explanation of only the **last question** she asked under Question A.
- **Yes?** — may be selected to learn in advance what follows from a final Yes result.
- **No?** — may be selected to learn in advance what follows from a final No result.
- **Deal** — allows a child graveyard card to be recovered if Borksen accepts a small request from Morena; Borksen may reject the request after hearing it, but then the turn advances and Deal goes to the graveyard. Deal's last possible use is when Borksen has two child cards remaining.

Chapter 407 does **not** supply the actual Aim answer, Power/ability explanation, Yes? consequence, No? consequence, Deal request, or any card outcome after play starts.

## No-cheating / answer-convention boundary

Borksen tests Morena by asking how she would answer the statement that she will not cheat. Morena uses the exchange to establish the response convention for negative questions, noting that cultures can map yes/no differently depending on whether the speaker or questioner is treated as the grammatical subject. Voconte jokes with Yokotani about the convention, and Yokotani apologizes for not realizing it could seem unusual.

Morena then answers from her own perspective: **No, she will not cheat**. Borksen becomes confident that Morena does not intend to cheat, reasoning from Morena's authority, her subordinates' behavior, and the respect shown during the negotiation. The archive records Morena's promise and Borksen's confidence. It does not convert that exchange into an unsupplied Nen-enforced anti-cheating mechanic or omniscient proof that cheating is physically impossible.

## Recruitment-interview inference boundary

Borksen infers that the negotiation game serves both as a recruitment rite and as an interview through which Morena can assess a candidate's personality, intelligence, and suitability. This remains Borksen's interpretation of the process rather than a separately narrated institutional rule.

## Final condition / stopping point

After Morena finishes explaining Deal, she asks Borksen whether she will play. Borksen agrees on one condition: **Borksen herself will decide which face-down child card is selected when Morena's side would otherwise make that choice**. Morena immediately accepts, says that this has happened before, and asks only whether Borksen is comfortable with Heil-Ly's side handling the shuffle. Morena then announces that the game will begin.
