# Chapter 397 source note

## Source boundary

The maintained Chapter 397 packet is based only on the user-supplied Hunterpedia Chapter 397 synopsis text supplied on 2026-08-10. No chapter title was supplied, so the archive stores no invented title.

Chapter 397 continues the undated pre-voyage Meteor City origin flashback begun in Chapter 395 and continued through Chapter 396. The supplied synopsis later includes an explicitly years-later coda in which the Spider is born. Publication order, historical story-time, and current Black Whale character/organization state remain separate.

No exact flashback year, exact date, exact clock time, or exact current age for Chrollo is invented. Chrollo asks Uvogin to wait three years and says he intends to have his power and a system for Meteor City prepared before he turns fourteen; the archive preserves those statements without deriving a more precise birthday chronology than the synopsis supplies.

No Chapter 398+ consequence or later retrospective explanation is backfilled.

## Sarasa is missing and the screening is canceled

At the All-Faiths Church, another Power Cleaners screening is scheduled, but Sarasa, Franklin, and Nobunaga are not yet present with fifteen minutes remaining. Sheila points out that Franklin and Nobunaga are habitually barely late while Sarasa normally is not.

Chrollo decides to look for Sarasa, and Sheila accompanies him because Chrollo does not know Sarasa's caretakers. One caretaker says she has not seen Sarasa since the previous day and had assumed Sarasa was with the others.

Back at the church, Phinks reports that Sarasa never returned to her hamlet. Uvogin suspects she may have gone searching for videos, and the group becomes visibly worried.

Chrollo goes before the waiting audience, cancels the performance, explains that Sarasa has been missing since the previous day, and reminds everyone about the recent child abductions around Meteor City. He blames himself for allowing Sarasa to leave alone and says the group will continue searching.

The archive records Chrollo's self-blame as his own judgment. It does not convert that statement into an objective causal finding that he caused Sarasa's disappearance.

## The community joins the search

Children in the audience volunteer to help search. Uvogin organizes them by hamlet, tells them to speak to caretakers, then seek the factory manager and adult help, and says that using Chrollo's name will make people cooperate.

Phinks tells everyone that they will find Orange and gives Uvogin the keys to his bike so Uvogin and Chrollo can search more quickly.

Uvogin tells the crying Chrollo that Sarasa is probably injured somewhere and unable to move. This is reassurance and hope before the discovery, not confirmed information about Sarasa's condition.

## Heart-shaped pouch, tracks, and Uga Forest

While searching, Chrollo finds Sarasa's heart-shaped pouch on the ground. He also notices tire tracks and footprints.

Chrollo looks toward Uga Forest and says Sarasa could be there. The archive stores this as Chrollo's evidence-based search inference. The tracks are not used to invent the number, identities, vehicle model, exact route, or exact sequence of the perpetrators.

## Sarasa's body is recovered

Inside Uga Forest, the group finds a garbage bag suspended from a tree branch and a note attached to the trunk with a knife.

Uvogin says they cannot know whether the bag contains Sarasa until someone checks. Chrollo volunteers. Uvogin takes the knife from the note and repeatedly asks what it says, while Chrollo tells him to get the bag down.

After Uvogin cuts the rope, the bag falls. Chrollo opens it and confirms the horrific outcome: Sarasa's dismembered body is inside, with another note covering her face.

At the Chapter 397 publication boundary, Sarasa's death is therefore confirmed. The archive updates the earlier Chapter 396 cliffhanger into a confirmed murder outcome.

### The note-content boundary

**The text of the killers' note is not supplied in the synopsis.**

Chrollo can read it but refuses to tell Uvogin what it says, even when Uvogin grabs him and demands an answer. Chrollo tells Uvogin to learn to read it himself.

The archive does not invent, reconstruct, quote, summarize, or infer the note's contents. It also does not infer the perpetrators' exact motive from an unread message.

## Grief and bringing Sarasa home

Uvogin becomes furious after Chrollo refuses to reveal the note and begins walking away. Machi challenges him over leaving Sarasa behind and says they have to take her home because it would be too sad to leave her there.

Machi holds the bag, apologizes to Sarasa, and speaks about how much pain and fear Sarasa must have experienced. Pakunoda and Sheila cry, with Sheila embracing her friend.

Machi's statements about Sarasa's fear and pain are expressions of grief and empathy. They are not used to reconstruct an unseen abduction sequence.

## Embalming and farewell

Later, Sarasa lies in a casket at the church. The children say she looks beautiful and almost alive.

Lisores explains embalming as a technique for restoring and preserving a body as close as possible to its living appearance. He says Sarasa's restoration is remarkable given the condition in which she was found and asks the children to thank the embalmer, Ms. Renko.

The children place flowers in Sarasa's casket. Chrollo returns her heart-shaped pouch. Uvogin places one of the tapes with her because the group has memorized it.

The archive treats the casket scene as a funeral/farewell and does not reinterpret the restored appearance as resurrection or consciousness continuation.

## Renko's special embalming ability

Machi approaches Renko and asks whether she can learn to embalm in the same way.

Renko says she cannot simply teach it because **her technique is a special ability**. Machi asks whether that ability is connected to why Sarasa's body is glowing so strongly, and Renko asks whether Machi can see aura.

The archive creates a descriptive ability record, **Renko's Embalming Ability**, because the synopsis explicitly identifies Renko's method as a special ability and connects the scene to visible aura.

The following remain unresolved at Chapter 397:

- the ability's official name;
- Renko's Nen category;
- activation procedure;
- whether physical embalming steps are also required;
- range and duration;
- aura cost;
- whether it can be used on every deceased body;
- limits imposed by injury, decomposition, time since death, or other conditions;
- whether it has any function beyond restoration/preservation of appearance.

The demonstrated target is Sarasa. The archive does not universalize one observed use into unlimited restoration mechanics.

## Machi sees aura

Machi's question about the glow and Renko's immediate response asking whether Machi can see aura establish that Machi is perceiving aura in this scene.

This is evidence of aura perception. It does **not** by itself establish Machi's Nen category, complete training state, Hatsu, teacher, mastery level, or the exact mechanism by which she learned to see aura.

Renko gives Machi a marked piece of paper and says she may visit if her caretakers permit it. Machi is told to take the last bus, ask for Kirimori Valley, and show the paper to ride there for free.

Kirimori Valley is stored as a named destination. The synopsis does not establish its exact coordinates, distance from Meteor City, political jurisdiction, or route geometry.

## Burial and Chrollo's three-year request

After Sarasa is buried in a nearby cemetery, Chrollo asks Uvogin to wait three years.

Chrollo says that within that period, and before he turns fourteen, he will prepare his own power and a proper system for Meteor City.

Uvogin initially rejects waiting and says he is leaving to avenge Sarasa. Chrollo argues that an immediate search would be like finding a needle in a haystack and reminds Uvogin of their earlier wish to travel the world together.

The archive records the three-year period and before-fourteen target exactly as supplied. It does not infer Chrollo's exact birthday or a precise calendar year.

## Chrollo's communication-network trap plan

Chrollo predicts an approaching communication revolution in which people around the world will be able to connect and share information almost instantly.

He reasons that Sarasa's crime scene was staged as a message designed to attract attention, compares it to theater, and points to cigarette butts near the scene as evidence that someone may have recorded it.

He further predicts that criminals who create such material will want to show their work to the world.

These are **Chrollo's deductions and predictions**. Chapter 397 does not independently confirm:

- who recorded the crime;
- that a recording definitely exists;
- which perpetrator smoked the cigarettes;
- the exact technology that will be used;
- the exact future network architecture;
- that every criminal of this type behaves the way Chrollo predicts.

Chrollo then explains his intended strategy: exploit Meteor City's existing legal/social void to create a place where criminals can evade ordinary police controls, draw such offenders into that space, and identify the people responsible among them.

Shalnark arrives and summarizes the idea as building a criminal hideout using the new technology so the group can search for the culprits within the resulting crowd.

At Chapter 397 this is a **plan**, not an already completed network.

## The group commits to preparation

Shalnark says the others will help and that three years will pass quickly. He says they need knowledge and means to approach the culprits as well as enough strength and skill to deal with them.

The other children emerge and join the discussion.

### Sheila boundary

The supplied synopsis depicts a downcast Sheila walking away through the forest while the others commit to Chrollo's plan.

The synopsis's editorial phrase that she “refuses to embrace the darkness” is not treated as direct character testimony. Chapter 397, as supplied here, does not give Sheila dialogue explaining why she walks away.

The archive therefore records only the observable facts: Sheila appears downcast and leaves the group during this discussion. Her exact motive, whether she explicitly rejects the revenge plan, and what she decides afterward remain unresolved unless later evidence establishes them.

## Chrollo crosses from theatrical villain to deliberate criminal identity

Chapter 396 kept Chrollo's frightening villain performance and Uvogin's “world's greatest villain” ambition inside theatrical context.

Chapter 397 changes that boundary.

Chrollo says the group must be prepared to sacrifice their lives to prevent more victims like Sarasa and the other children of Meteor City.

He then says that in three years he will kill many people. He acknowledges that Sarasa would not have wanted that and says he will apologize if he ever sees her again, but he refuses to let the perpetrators escape under a legal system he does not believe will make them repent.

Finally, Chrollo says he will live the rest of his life as a villain and make the world shudder at the thought of him. He says he will design Meteor City and himself so that predators like Sarasa's killers will not approach them.

These statements are now an explicit criminal/villain program. The Chapter 396 acting boundary remains correct **at Chapter 396**, but it must not be carried forward to erase Chapter 397's deliberate ideological transition.

Chrollo's claims about what the legal system will or will not accomplish are his judgments, not archive-certified conclusions about every legal outcome.

## Leadership nomination and the Spider

Chrollo asks Uvogin to help him and asks whether Uvogin will become the Troupe's leader.

Uvogin refuses the leadership role and says Chrollo is the head. He says that if Chrollo is the head, he will follow him until he dies.

Uvogin points toward Chrollo, and the other seven shown members agree to nominate Chrollo as leader.

The archive therefore records **Chrollo's leadership nomination** at Chapter 397. It does not label Uvogin as the Troupe's leader.

The synopsis then moves years later and describes Chrollo addressing members as **the Spider is born**. At this boundary, the historical transformation into the criminal Spider organization is established.

The synopsis links this Troupe to the Phantom Troupe, so the canonical organization entity may be attached to the years-later founding coda. However, the supplied synopsis does not independently reproduce the precise spoken wording of the complete group-name coinage. The archive therefore distinguishes the confirmed Spider/Phantom Troupe organization identity from the unresolved exact naming utterance or naming mechanism.
