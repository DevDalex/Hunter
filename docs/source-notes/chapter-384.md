# Chapter 384 source boundaries

Reviewed: 2026-08-09

Sole story source for the dedicated Chapter 384 upgrade: user-supplied Hunterpedia Chapter 384 synopsis.

## Publication order versus in-story chronology

Chapter 384 follows Chapter 383 in publication order, so it belongs after Chapter 383 in the maintained chapter-research chain.

Its story opens as a flashback explicitly set at **10:30 p.m. on Voyage Day 7**, before the Sunday banquet material reached in Chapters 382–383. The archive therefore keeps two independent facts intact:

- maintained publication order: 383 → 384;
- in-story Chapter 384 opening time: Voyage Day 7, 10:30 p.m.

The opening Cha-R scene receives that exact timestamp. Later Chapter 384 scenes remain inside the same flashback sequence, but the supplied synopsis does not provide a second exact clock time, so no later timestamp is invented.

## Missing workers and the Tier 3 hitman

Ken’i Wang reports:

- eight missing Cha-R men;
- similar Xi-Yu disappearances;
- more than 300 missing workers overall.

Ken’i believes the killer responsible for the Tier 3 civilian murders has moved downward with accomplices and is continuing to kill on the lowest tier.

The disappearances are reported facts within the Chapter 384 conversation. The proposed movement of the killer and accomplices is **Ken’i’s theory** and is stored as such.

## Phantom Troupe deductions

Phinks says Hisoka was not found on Tier 5. This is a negative search result, not proof of Hisoka’s location elsewhere.

Phinks also makes several deductions:

- the door used by Ken’i and Tajao may connect to a higher tier;
- the hitman appears interested in that door;
- Brocco Li may be a target.

These are preserved as Phinks’s hypotheses. Chapter 384 does not convert them into confirmed route topology or a confirmed assassination plan against Brocco Li.

## Kakin mafia hit-and-raid settlement procedure

Ken’i and Tajao explicitly explain the operating procedure used by the Kakin mafia families for inter-family hits and raids.

The supplied synopsis establishes the following Chapter 384 rule set:

- a member making a hit or raid is expected to inform their boss;
- the family bosses then negotiate to smooth over the incident;
- Tajao states that this must be done within twenty-four hours of the hit or raid;
- the initiating boss must have been away from the family’s home turf when the initial incident occurred;
- in the scenario under discussion, Tiers 1 and 2 count as away from the lower-tier home turf;
- Tajao says the two-day delay can still be reconciled if Morena was in Tier 1 or Tier 2;
- Tajao says that if Morena was in Tier 3 and still has not made the required call, all-out war becomes inevitable.

The archive stores this as the **Kakin mafia shipboard procedure presented by Ken’i and Tajao**. It is not generalized into a universal rule about organized crime outside this story system.

## Phantom Troupe and prospective Heil-Ly war

Tajao tells the Spiders that killing the hitman would put the Phantom Troupe itself at war with Heil-Ly and would cost the Troupe information and assistance from ordinary lower-level criminals.

Nobunaga accepts the consequence and proposes killing Morena. Phinks then prioritizes gathering the other Spiders and hunting the hitman first while Morena’s location is verified.

The archive therefore distinguishes:

1. a stated procedural consequence: attacking the hitman would create direct Troupe–Heil-Ly hostility;
2. the Troupe’s willingness to accept that risk;
3. Nobunaga’s proposed Morena attack;
4. completed violence, which has **not** yet occurred merely because it was proposed in this briefing.

## Second-track Fakers

Chapter 384 identifies **Onior Longbao, Brocco Li, and Morena Prudo** as “Second-track Fakers.”

The supplied synopsis describes them as illegitimate heirs to the throne who are allowed to live and compensated covertly so long as they remain outside the spotlight and obey the monarchy system absolutely.

This classification is stored as supplied. The Chapter 384 integration does not invent additional parentage, succession eligibility, or hidden genealogy beyond what the maintained archive already supports.

## Morena’s location

The chapter supplies two direct observations/statements:

- Morena’s Tier 1 room is shown empty;
- Tserriednich says she is not in the VVIP area.

Tserriednich then says she has **probably** been in the lower tiers since boarding and hidden with help from her butler.

That lower-tier history is Tserriednich’s estimate, not independent location confirmation. The archive therefore records Morena as absent from the observed Tier 1/VVIP location while leaving her exact current location unresolved.

Tserriednich’s order for lower-tier soldiers to search for her hideout is recorded as a confirmed operational response.

## Tserriednich’s quarters

Guards are shown cleaning blood, hair, and bodily remains while several weapons are present.

The supplied synopsis does not identify the victims, establish who caused the scene, or explain the weapons’ exact relationship to the remains. No identities or causal sequence are invented.

## Two distinct Nen beasts around Tserriednich

This distinction is mandatory.

Chapter 384 shows Tserriednich with:

1. the **Guardian Spirit Beast** produced by the Seed Urn Ceremony, already established before this chapter;
2. a **second Nen beast** that Theta identifies as a product of Tserriednich’s own Specialist Nen.

Theta says the second beast was created instinctively and without Tserriednich consciously intending to create it. She calls it his **alter ego**.

The archive uses the descriptive internal label **Tserriednich’s Instinctive Specialist Nen Beast** because the supplied material does not provide an official ability name.

Chapter 384 confirms:

- the second beast exists;
- it is distinct from the Seed Urn Guardian Spirit Beast;
- Theta identifies its origin with Tserriednich’s Specialist Nen;
- its creation was instinctive and unintended;
- Theta describes it as an alter ego.

Chapter 384 does **not** establish:

- a formal official name;
- a complete ability;
- targets;
- costs;
- range;
- duration;
- a consciousness-transfer or independent-personhood mechanic;
- its relationship to abilities revealed in later chapters.

No Chapter 385+ mechanic is backfilled into the Chapter 384 boundary.

## Guardian Spirit Beast reaction to Theta

During the Zetsu lesson, Tserriednich’s mare-like Guardian Spirit Beast becomes suspicious of Theta. Theta reasons that she has not lied and that the training is genuinely necessary, tells the beast to move back, and it does.

This scene is stored as observed behavior. Chapter 384 by itself is not used to manufacture a complete universal lie-detection rule beyond what earlier maintained evidence already establishes.

## Zetsu training

Theta reviews the Four Major Principles: Ten, Zetsu, Ren, and Hatsu.

She says Tserriednich has already been practicing Ten and Ren at a high level, that daily practice is needed to improve Hatsu, and that mastery of Zetsu is key to mastering Nen.

She instructs him to close his aura in sections, then across his entire body, and maintain the full state for more than an hour. Tserriednich begins the exercise.

The archive records this as the Chapter 384 training state without importing later results of that Zetsu practice.

## Theta’s “tomorrow” statement

Theta privately thinks that she can do **“it” tomorrow** if necessary.

The supplied synopsis does not define “it.” The archive therefore stores an unresolved next-day intention and does not name the planned action.
