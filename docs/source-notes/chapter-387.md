## Demonstrated activation

Theta instructs Tserriednich to close his eyes and maintain Zetsu for one hour while ignoring her interruptions.

Once Tserriednich fully closes off his aura, he sees static followed by an overhead future scene.

After he loses Zetsu and opens his eyes, Theta immediately repeats the exact dialogue he heard in the vision. Tserriednich therefore identifies the scene as being **ten seconds ahead**.

The archive records the demonstrated activation as:

- eyes closed;
- fully entered Zetsu;
- static;
- ten-second-ahead vision.

It does not add unsupplied activation requirements.

## The vision can continue beyond the first ten seconds

On the second test, Tserriednich initially worries that he may have to remain defenseless for ten seconds merely to obtain ten seconds of future sight.

He then discovers that this early model is incomplete.

If he maintains the eyes-closed Zetsu state, the future vision continues **past the initial ten seconds** while real time also advances.

His initial defenseless-for-ten-seconds concern is therefore preserved as an early hypothesis, not promoted into the final ability limitation.

Chapter 387 does not supply an ultimate maximum duration for continuous viewing.

## Present awareness and future visual information coexist

While watching the continuing future sequence, Tserriednich can still feel Theta's current presence in real time while visually observing the Theta who is ten seconds ahead.

## Forecast divergence and Theta's perception

The coffee-cup experiment is the key mechanics test.

Future Theta picks up and drops a coffee cup intending to disrupt Tserriednich's Zetsu. Tserriednich deliberately maintains the state instead of following the behavior shown in the forecast.

Theta nevertheless reacts according to the forecast sequence and perceives Tserriednich as the version shown there.

The assassination attempt then demonstrates the same principle operationally:

- Tserriednich sees future Theta draw her gun;
- he moves his actual body away from the forecast position;
- he knows Theta will still perceive him at the forecast position;
- Theta fires at that perceived version;
- Tserriednich physically survives.

The archive therefore treats Tserriednich's ability to diverge his real actions while the demonstrated observer continues through the forecast perception as confirmed Chapter 387 mechanics.

It does **not** convert one demonstrated interaction into unsupplied universal claims about every observer configuration, every Nen user, or every possible countermeasure.
