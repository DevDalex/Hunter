- **Title policy:** no chapter title was supplied in the source text, so the archive does not invent one.

## Laundry-room uncertainty

Chapter 399 opens exactly where Chapter 398 stopped: Hinrigh and Nobunaga are looking into the laundry-filled room.

Hinrigh feels that people were present moments earlier. Nobunaga realizes that if Heil-Ly expected wall breaches, the neighboring rooms could have been cooperating with them and could have warned the hideout after the Troupe used those neighbors to test traps.

Hinrigh offers several alternatives:

- the civilians may have been restrained and taken elsewhere;
- Heil-Ly may have a rule to evacuate when one of its neighboring rooms is teleported into the hideout;
- oblivious neighbors may be deliberately useful as cheap warning devices rather than active accomplices.

The archive preserves all of these as **speaker-level hypotheses**. Chapter 399 does not establish which explanation is correct, whether the civilians were knowingly cooperating, whether they were restrained, or whether an automatic evacuation protocol exists.

## Damage: “Sweet Home”

Chapter 399 formally reveals Terebellum’s **Emission** ability, **Damage: “Sweet Home”**.

Directly demonstrated / explained mechanics:

1. Hinrigh throws a knife into Terebellum’s head.
2. Terebellum remains standing and removes the knife.
3. The blade/tip is found in the drink can he was holding.
4. Later, Nobunaga stabs Yokotani while Terebellum has his **right hand** on Yokotani’s shoulder.
5. The katana tip disappears from the attack path and Terebellum bears the intercepted injury.
6. The chapter explains that damage to something Terebellum is touching with his **right hand at the moment the damage is received** is taken into Terebellum.
7. He can transfer received damage onward to something he touches with his **left hand**.
8. If he does not transfer the received damage onward, Terebellum bears it himself.
9. The demonstrated transfer can include displacement of the attacking object/material together with the damage.

The maintained ability record does **not** invent:

- a maximum damage capacity;
- a transfer range beyond the demonstrated contact rules;
- a delayed-storage duration;
- a healing function;
- unconditional immunity;
- an aura-cost formula;
- or a complete rule for every possible self-targeted attack.

Nobunaga’s earlier thought that Terebellum could be an “interceptor-type” is preserved as his pre-reveal analysis, not as a separate formal Nen category.

## Perigord, Orarge, Soufflé, and “organ” / kikan

When Hinrigh throws another knife, Terebellum avoids it and moves Soufflé out of the way. Perigord appears willing to receive the attack and wonders how long the injury would take to heal, but Orarge blocks the knife with an issue of *Weekly Shōnen Jump*.

That scene does **not** reveal Perigord’s personal ability. The archive does not infer a healing ability, category, name, or mechanic from Perigord’s thought about recovery time.

Likewise, Orarge blocking the knife with the magazine is not promoted into a newly revealed Nen ability.

Orarge explicitly reminds Perigord that he has already been chosen as the **“organ” (kikan)** and that Morena told him to keep his head down. Chapter 399 confirms the selection and Morena’s instruction, but it does not explain the role’s exact function. The maintained boundary therefore leaves “organ” unresolved.

Soufflé says the members were told not to fight there and leaves the immediate room. Her exact next room is not supplied.

## A Battle of Wits: “LSDF”

Chapter 399 formally reveals Yokotani’s **Conjuration** ability, **A Battle of Wits: “LSDF”**.

The chapter explicitly supplies these conditions and mechanics:

- the ability can only be used at the **hideout where Morena is located**;
- Yokotani activates it after identifying himself to an intruder who has broken the law;
- the more serious the intruder’s crime, the higher the level of guards Yokotani can create;
- the conjured guards cannot harm the criminal;
- the criminal’s attacks are ineffective against the guards once the conditions apply;
- seven guards appear in the demonstrated use;
- Hinrigh and Nobunaga are charged with trespassing and attempted murder and the guards begin at alert level 4;
- after Nobunaga attacks one guard, it reaches maximum alert and its aura increases;
- the guards can restrain, confiscate a weapon, carry a target away, and enforce expulsion without directly injuring the criminal.

Nobunaga calls the guards **autopilot**. Hinrigh additionally reasons that because they are automatic, Yokotani will not be able to see what Hinrigh does inside the laundry room. The archive distinguishes these two claims: the automatic behavior is directly stated by Nobunaga from the encounter, while Yokotani’s lack of visual access through the guards remains Hinrigh’s tactical inference rather than an independently narrated rule.

The maintained record does not invent the full crime-to-level table, complete alert-level scale, maximum number of guards, maximum aura, exact range, duration, or aura cost.

## Morena’s location boundary

LSDF is explicitly stated to be usable only at the **hideout where Morena is located**, and Yokotani activates it during this Chapter 399 confrontation.

This materially advances the Chapter 398 boundary: Morena is now supported as being somewhere within this qualifying Heil-Ly hideout/complex at this time.

It does **not** establish:

- that Morena is in Yokotani’s room specifically;
- that Morena personally operates the Room 3101 teleport route;

## LSDF expulsion and the Room 3101 spatial route

The LSDF guards restrain Nobunaga, confiscate his katana, carry him out, and he is eventually teleported away. Yokotani confirms his expulsion. Nobunaga reappears in **Room 3101**.

Hinrigh later also returns from the hideout to Room 3101.

Together with Chapter 398’s direct evidence, Chapter 399 now supports this operational pattern:

- **Room 3101-side / tested front-door inward crossing → Heil-Ly hideout**
- **hideout-side exit / expulsion → Room 3101**

Nobunaga therefore describes the arrangement as serving both as a trap and a secret passage.

The archive does **not** conclude that LSDF itself creates or owns this pre-existing spatial route merely because Nobunaga’s expulsion uses it. The route’s formal ability name, user, ability-specific Nen category, switch geometry, and exact relationship to Gateaume or Voconte remain unresolved.

Nobunaga further reasons that Heil-Ly must have a separate **member-only jump point** somewhere in the room. No such member-only jump point is directly demonstrated in the supplied Chapter 399 synopsis, so that remains a hypothesis.

## Hinrigh’s transmitter and Biohazard resource state

While the LSDF guards pursue him, Hinrigh retreats to the laundry room, closes the door, induces himself to vomit, retrieves the still-transformed transmitter-oyster, and hides it beneath a cabinet.

At the Chapter 399 endpoint, the transmitter is still visibly in **oyster form** beneath that cabinet and beeps twice.

After returning to Room 3101, Hinrigh says:

These are maintained as two separate current resource statements.

The Biohazard record therefore gains a **rest-of-day availability limit for Hinrigh in Chapter 399**, but the archive does not infer:

Chapter 398’s roughly two-hour transmitter estimate, size-dependent duration statement, aura-depletion reversion statement, and receiver behavior remain intact.

## Main-base assessment

While evading the LSDF guards, Hinrigh becomes confident that they have found Heil-Ly’s main base because of the teleportation traps and powerful defensive users.

The archive preserves that as Hinrigh’s strongly supported field assessment. It does not transform his conclusion into an exact ship coordinate or a complete solved floor plan. The group still intends to use the ship’s floor plan, witnesses, member descriptions, and the transmitter to narrow the location further.

The archive preserves this as an assessment of Heil-Ly’s coordination and unresolved capabilities. It does **not** invent a separate mastermind, identify an unknown coordinator, or resolve Morena’s personal ability set beyond what the chapter actually supplies.

## Trivia / intertextual references

The supplied trivia is maintained only as reference metadata and does not change story mechanics:

- the Hunter × Hunter lettering on Yokotani’s book is said to refer to the Six Codes legal-code tradition;
- the subtitle *Sweet Home* references the 1989 Japanese horror film and its video-game adaptation, which later influenced *Resident Evil*;
- the Japanese title associated with Yokotani’s ability, *Bokkō*, references Ken’ichi Sakemi’s historical novel and its Mohist themes.

No Nen rule, legal power, character motive, or hidden plot fact is inferred from those references.
