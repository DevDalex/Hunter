# Chapter 410 source note

## Source boundary

The modern Chapter 410 packet uses the current user-supplied Hunterpedia-style synopsis and included notes provided on 2026-08-12 as its sole substantive Chapter 410 story source. The previously maintained packet already carried the title **Negotiation: Part 4** and Japanese title **交渉④** from user-supplied Hunterpedia material; those metadata fields are retained rather than re-fetched from another source.

Chapter 410 remains on Voyage Day 12 and immediately continues Borksen’s intentional Yes from the Chapter 409 endpoint. The chapter later gives an explicit relative-time marker: **forty minutes have passed since Special Martial Law was declared**. No new absolute clock minute is supplied.

The strict stopping point is the Justice Bureau confrontation after Botobai notices Benjamin’s pallor and Kaiser privately considers Benjamin’s possible infection, a one-day delay strategy, and an unidentified final stronghold. **No Chapter 411+ consequence, movement, death, legal resolution, or later explanation is imported backward.**

## Borksen final-Yes / anti-cheating boundary

Morena demands that Borksen confirm Yes one final time and warns that the answer cannot be changed. Borksen outwardly confirms Yes while her internal thoughts explicitly resist and insist that she does not want that choice.

Morena then accuses Borksen of cheating and reconstructs the tactic as follows:

- while checking the untouched No and Return cards after Deal, Borksen placed a tiny indentation along both front-side edges of Return;
- the mark was positioned beyond Morena’s view;
- Orarge would see only the card backs while shuffling and therefore would not notice the front-side mark;
- Morena interprets Borksen crushing Return after the winning reveal as destruction of the evidence.

The archive records the physical cheating reconstruction as **Morena’s stated explanation**.

Morena further reveals that the negotiation game and cards are part of her ability. She says cheating automatically triggers a **Manipulation** component that restricts the opponent to choosing only **Yes or No**. Borksen’s outward Yes while internally resisting is therefore recorded as a demonstrated coercive effect of the Chapter 410 game, not as proof that Borksen voluntarily wanted to join Morena’s cause.

The archive does not generalize this game-specific anti-cheating rule into a universal Nen law.

## Level 0 / joining boundary

After Borksen reaffirms that Yes means she will join Heil-Ly, the surrounding members applaud and the coercive state releases.

Morena reminds Borksen that full joining still depends on the three conditions already stated in Chapter 409:

1. the game ends with Yes;
2. Morena infects the recruit through a kiss;
3. the recruit is present when Morena or another Heil-Ly member commits murder.

Chapter 410 explicitly places Borksen at **Level 0**. Morena says she becomes formal **Level 1** only after the remaining murder-presence condition is fulfilled.

The archive therefore distinguishes:

- accepted on Heil-Ly’s side under the final Yes: confirmed;
- kiss condition: completed in Chapter 409;
- murder-presence condition: not completed in the supplied Chapter 410 synopsis;
- current level: Level 0;
- formal Level 1 membership: not yet reached.

Borksen is not silently promoted to Level 1 or treated as ideologically converted to Morena’s destruction goal.

## Installed-game / surveillance boundary

Morena describes a game application installed in Borksen and says she manages its play data. Morena explicitly says she can always know Borksen’s:

- level;
- points;
- location;
- status.

Morena also gives three stated termination routes: the game is cleared, the administrator ceases to exist, or the “phone” is destroyed.

The supplied synopsis says Morena reveals the ability’s name during this explanation but does **not reproduce that name**. The archive therefore does not invent or backfill a new name from this synopsis alone.

Borksen separately decides to assume the installed system is spyware capable of transmitting everything she sees and hears. That wider audiovisual surveillance model is preserved strictly as **Borksen’s defensive assumption**. Morena confirms only level, points, location, and status tracking in the supplied synopsis.

## Borksen counter-strategy boundary

Borksen recognizes that her usefulness may expire if Heil-Ly finds another Specialist candidate. She decides she needs power to oppose them while also recognizing that killing is the quickest route to ability development inside their system.

Borksen tells Morena she intends to return to her original companions. Morena answers that Heil-Ly are now also her people. Before leaving, Borksen asks Morena to show her around so she can learn more about her new comrades.

The archive records Borksen as operationally aligned under the forced Yes while maintaining underlying resistance and information-gathering intent. Her exact counter-strategy remains unresolved.

## Tier 3 / Room 3101 Case S boundary

Tier 3 remains under Special Martial Law. Civilians are ordered to remain still until searched and are threatened with being shot for noncompliance.

Military personnel report a **Case S** at Room 3101. The room is sealed and guarded after **three soldiers enter one after another and vanish immediately**. The investigators call this a textbook supernatural incident.

The supplied synopsis also reports a concealed bathroom route/trick wall associated with the investigation.

### Room-number correction

The user-supplied Chapter 410 notes state that an original reference to **Room 125** was erroneous and that a Togashi X post dated 2024-12-21 said the volume release corrects the intended room reference to **Room 3101**.

For the modern archive packet, **Room 3101 is canonical** and Room 125 is not created as a second Chapter 410 navigable location or route. The correction is recorded as source-boundary metadata rather than preserving a known erroneous room number as parallel canon.

The notes also say that the missing corporal mentioned by investigators could possibly be Maizan but that this is **not confirmed**. The archive therefore keeps the missing corporal unidentified.

The exact ability, user, activation trigger, and route mechanism behind the Room 3101 disappearances remain unresolved at the Chapter 410 boundary.

## Benjamin prince-search boundary

Benjamin receives a report of a suspected Heil-Ly hideout involving Nen, with the room empty and searches for other locations ongoing. He assigns Squad 3 to the Heil-Ly problem while prioritizing prince security.

Benjamin identifies Zhang Lei and Luzurus as currently unaccounted for and orders them located before lower-ranking princes.

A soldier reports that Zhang Lei moved toward Tier 2 shortly before Special Martial Law through a cross-bridge inside Xi-Yu boss Onior’s residence. The route is being verified and followed, with a follow-up expected in fifteen minutes.

Benjamin thinks Zhang Lei likely sought the Justice Bureau and may have gone farther down to meet the mafia, then redirects Squad 2 reconnaissance and scouts toward him. The archive separates the **reported cross-bridge movement** from **Benjamin’s inferred destination and motive**.

Luzurus’s location is not supplied in this synopsis.

## Benjamin disease-clock / emergency succession boundary

On the way to the Justice Bureau, Benjamin internally calculates that once symptoms appear he will fall into a coma in ten hours and die two hours later. He estimates roughly **9.5 usable hours remain** and plans to eliminate the remaining princes before that operational window closes.

Benjamin hopes to obtain official succession rights in time to pass the throne to his child if he dies. He frames that strategy through **Article 1 of the State of Emergency Act: “Crisis of Preserving the Monarchy.”**

The archive records the 9.5-hour figure as **Benjamin’s own internal calculation**, not as a separately validated medical prognosis.

## Justice Bureau / biological-terrorism allegation boundary

Benjamin arrives at the Justice Bureau and tells Mizaistom, Botobai, and Kaiser that he intends to consolidate all three branches of government by military force at the central court, use the Justice Bureau as a joint-force command center, and make the three officials witnesses.

Mizaistom asks for the legal basis of Special Martial Law and Nasubi’s authorization letter.

Benjamin states that his basis is biological terrorism allegedly orchestrated by Tserriednich and Halkenburg using a developing biological weapon sample brought aboard through a collaborator in Benjamin’s military. He says multiple co-conspirators have been arrested and questioned.

The archive preserves this as **Benjamin’s stated allegation and legal justification**. It is not promoted to an independently confirmed account.

Mizaistom privately concludes that Benjamin intends to use secured princes as hostages to seize the three branches and judges most of Benjamin’s presentation to be conveniently twisted half-truths. Those are preserved as **Mizaistom’s analysis**, not narrator-confirmed findings.

Benjamin says suspected infected people cannot leave isolation until the sample is fully analyzed and that hospital transfer cannot proceed while governmental power remains unintegrated. He also says Nasubi’s authorization letter is still being written and can be retrieved only after signature and seal.

## Botobai / Kaiser inference boundary

Botobai directly notices that Benjamin looks pale and asks whether he is alright. Benjamin says there is no problem.

Kaiser privately wonders whether Benjamin himself is infected and, using Halkenburg’s progression as a comparison, estimates that Benjamin may have no more than twenty-four hours. Kaiser hopes Special Martial Law can be delayed for one more day and thinks he still has a final stronghold.

The archive distinguishes:

- Benjamin’s pallor: observed;
- Benjamin’s denial of a problem: stated;
- Benjamin infection: Kaiser’s inference;
- twenty-four-hour ceiling: Kaiser’s inference;
- Kaiser’s final stronghold: existence as his private thought is supplied, but its identity and mechanics are unresolved.
