# Chapter 394 source note

## Source boundary

The maintained Chapter 394 packet is based only on the user-supplied Hunterpedia Chapter 394 synopsis text supplied on 2026-08-10. No chapter title was supplied, so the archive stores no invented title.

Chapter 394 directly continues the Voyage Day 10 Room 3101 and lower-tier Heil-Ly threads from Chapter 393. No exact Chapter 394 clock time is supplied, so none is inferred.

No Chapter 395+ explanation, identity reveal, ability rule, route mechanic, or consequence is backfilled.

## Gateaume and the Room 3101 body

The old man at Room 3101 is Gateaume. Hinrigh throws a knife into the displayed body’s right thigh. Gateaume reacts as though injured, but Hinrigh points out that there is **no blood** and asks where Gateaume’s **real body** is.

Gateaume says he used to be an insurance scammer, refuses to disclose his real body or Maizan, and then disappears. The knife falls to the floor.

The archive therefore records a descriptive **Gateaume’s Decoy Body Ability** entry, but only as a label for the observed phenomenon. Chapter 394 does **not** establish whether the displayed body is conjured, emitted, manipulated, projected, transformed, remotely controlled, or produced by another Nen mechanism. Nen category, activation, range, real-body location, sensory link, damage transfer, duration, and reset rules remain unresolved.

## Tassi and Room 3101

Tassi walks into Room 3101 to retrieve Hinrigh’s knife and disappears in front of Hinrigh, Ken’i, and the other mafia personnel.

Hinrigh initially suspects the same party responsible for earlier disappearances. Ken’i reminds him that Luini has already been killed by Nobunaga and that Luini’s head and torso remain at the Cha-R office. The archive therefore does not attribute the active Chapter 394 Room 3101 route to living Luini.

Tassi later appears in front of a closed door inside Heil-Ly’s network. This, together with Morena later saying that they can **no longer use Room 3101**, confirms at Chapter 394 that Room 3101 was part of Heil-Ly’s usable route/access system.

That confirmation does not establish a complete people-only teleportation rule, a specific trigger, the responsible user, directionality, or a full topology.

Maizan’s location and condition remain unresolved in Chapter 394.

## Bille, Matvere, and level 21

Bille ambushes Tassi and stabs him in the neck. Tassi is recorded as **dead**.

Bille celebrates reaching **level 21**. Matvere gives him a Water Divination cup. Matvere says that he himself tested as a **Transmuter**. Bille hopes to be an Emitter but tests as a **Conjurer**.

This demonstrates the level-21 transition discussed in Chapter 393 and confirms Bille’s and Matvere’s natural Nen types. It does **not** reveal Bille’s eventual personal ability, so no individual Bille ability is invented.

Gelato’s remark about many left-handed Conjurers is retained as her observation rather than a universal Nen rule.

## Terebellum, Chiffon Toto, Montblanc Toto, and processing

Chapter 394 explicitly discloses:

- Terebellum: level 21, smuggler / hitman, **Emitter**;
- Chiffon Toto: level 6, seamstress / ornament factory worker, **Conjurer**;
- Montblanc Toto: level 3, animal feed factory worker / body-disposal role, **Conjurer**.

Terebellum delivers Tassi’s corpse and says that Voconte directly connected the room to the processing area. Chiffon begins removing the corpse’s clothes while Montblanc prepares to dismember it.

The archive records this as an explicit Heil-Ly corpse-processing workflow.

## Voconte’s door ability

Chapter 393 only established Voconte’s proposed use of an unnamed door ability as a trap. Chapter 394 advances that knowledge: Terebellum says Voconte **directly connected the room to the processing area**.

This supports a direct spatial connection function. It still does not supply the official ability name, ability-specific Nen category, setup method, trigger, directionality, valid surfaces, maximum number of links, range, capacity, duration, aura cost, or reset rules.

Voconte is a confirmed natural **Emitter**, but the archive still does not automatically classify the door technique itself as Emission.

The archive also does not attribute every Room 3101, Door C, Room 3131, laundry, shower/processing, or living-room transition to Voconte unless the chapter explicitly does so.

## Morena’s response to Tserriednich

A Heil-Ly member tells Morena that the butler left in the VVIP area as liaison to the Fourth Prince has gone quiet. Morena considers that Tserriednich may be on to them and believes the hideout will be found soon based on Gateaume’s report.

Morena expects Tserriednich to want to deal with her personally for betrayal, but explicitly says that when and how he will move is unclear while the succession contest continues.

She plans to capture one of Tserriednich’s personal soldiers and **infect someone on his side with Contagion so that she can track his movements**. The archive records the intended tracking function exactly to that extent. Tracking precision, interface, range, duration, target awareness, and other limits are not supplied.

## Room 3101, Door C, Room 3131, and internal route reporting

Morena explicitly says they can no longer use **Room 3101** and should use **Door C**.

She sends Soufflé and Notre back to **Room 3131** to report on the situation in Room 3101. Notre says he returned to the hideout using 3131 and then the disposal area, described as the laundry room. To reverse the route, he explains that they must go through the laundry room to the processing area, described as the shower room, and then out through the living room.

The archive preserves this reported sequence. It does not invent omitted door triggers, assign every transition to Voconte, or convert the partial descriptions into a fully solved spatial map.

## Hinrigh’s camcorder-cat surveillance

Hinrigh returns to the large fountain, recovers the cat, turns it back into a camcorder, and reviews the footage.

This is retained as an additional demonstrated Biohazard surveillance/reversion use. Chapter 394 does not supply a new maximum duration, remote-control rule, transformation category, or other general mechanic beyond the shown recovery/reversion.

## Gipper’s Tier 3 operation and the discovered body

Tserriednich’s soldiers force entry into a registered Heil-Ly office on Tier 3 and discover an old body that appears to be a Cha-R member.

Corporal Gipper constructs an official story that a Heil-Ly member resisted arrest, fired on them, and escaped into the room, thereby justifying the forced entry. He worries that treating a supposed civilian death incorrectly could turn the mafia conflict into a wider political problem.

Gipper prioritizes capturing Morena before Tserriednich has to deal with her personally, plans to sweep Tier 3 with Xi-Yu and Cha-R help, wants to avoid unnecessary Royal Army/police attention, and limits the soldiers to handguns.

The archive distinguishes Gipper’s planned cover story from established truth about how the body died.

## Otocin, Nen, and Borksen’s transfer

Otocin asks what they should do if Heil-Ly has Nen users. Gipper is initially unfamiliar with the term.

Otocin describes Nen only as a dangerous power spreading on Tier 1 and says he learned about it from Borksen, who had been serving on royal security detail as a provisional Hunter. Gipper requests Borksen’s transfer to Tier 3 as an adviser.

Otocin’s statements do not establish detailed Nen mechanics or Nen-user status for him or Gipper.

## Tserriednich’s old firearm story

The soldiers recount an academy story in which Tserriednich mastered a firearm disassembly/reassembly drill and later shot a classmate in the leg after an argument.

The archive retains it as reported personal-history context from the soldier group. It is not used to infer a Nen mechanic.

## Borksen, Theta, Salkov, and classified Nen knowledge

Borksen rejoins the soldiers and says that Tserriednich is learning Nen from **Theta**, his personal bodyguard. She warns that leaking the information would result in immediate demotion.

Borksen herself says Nen is classified and that she does **not** know its details. She had intended to investigate before being transferred.

She says Theta and Salkov began acting strangely after the emergency broadcast and concludes that Morena is most likely a Nen user. She recommends avoiding Morena to preserve their lives.

This does not backfill detailed Parallel Future, Guardian Spirit Beast, Theta-assassination, or other knowledge into Borksen or the lower-tier soldiers beyond what Chapter 394 explicitly gives them.

## Tattoo-database exposure and survival planning

Borksen explains that the tattoo artist who gave their military unit its tattoos used to be a Heil-Ly member. Tserriednich removed him before Morena’s inauguration massacre, but the soldiers were listed in his client database. Borksen therefore believes Morena likely has information identifying the personal soldiers.

She compares Nen’s apparent danger to a top-secret weapon based on Theta and Salkov’s reactions, makes survival the priority, recommends establishing a working relationship with Xi-Yu or Cha-R, and agrees that the group needs a contingency plan if one of them is captured.

No later Borksen capture, Heil-Ly negotiation, recruitment, or Contagion outcome is imported into Chapter 394.
