# Chapter 378 source note

Source basis: user-supplied Hunterpedia Chapter 378 synopsis and chapter notes.

## Voyage-day placement

The supplied Chapter 378 material does not state a new voyage-day marker.

The maintained chronology places the chapter on **Voyage Day 4** as an immediate continuation from Chapter 377, but records that placement as inferred continuity rather than an independently supplied timestamp.

## Ken'i Wang reward wording

The supplied synopsis describes Ken'i Wang first mentioning **10 million** for useful information and then immediately revising his offer to **10 million for the person who finds Hisoka plus 10 million for the leader of that area**.

The supplied chapter notes separately list:

- 10 million for useful information;
- 10 million for finding Hisoka;
- 10 million for the leader of the area where he is found.

The maintained research preserves the chapter-note three-part reward structure and does not infer that the information reward was cancelled.

## Luini's level wording

The supplied notes state that Luini kills twenty civilians in Tier 3 and later a Cha-R guard in Tier 5, **gaining 21 levels and a transportation Nen ability in the process**.

The supplied material does not separately state Luini's starting level in this chapter. The maintained research therefore records:

- twenty-one kills attributed to Luini;
- a reported gain of twenty-one levels;
- crossing the level-20 ability threshold;
- acquisition of a transportation Nen ability;
- no independently calculated final current level.

The ability's official name and complete mechanics are not supplied and are not invented.

## Tier 3 witness reliability

The Tier 3 witness describes the killer as a man in his twenties, approximately 185–190 cm tall, with a crescent scar on his left cheek and disguised as Royal Army personnel.

Investigators explicitly caution that parts of the testimony may be confused because of shock. Chapter 378 later shows Luini matching the crescent-scar description, and the supplied notes identify Luini as the killer, but the archive continues to distinguish the witness's uncertain details from the confirmed killer attribution.
