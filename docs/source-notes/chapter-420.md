## Continuation from Chapter 419

Chapter 420 begins at the Chapter 419 endpoint inside the **Tier 1 VVIP casino** during Special Martial Law. Tier 1 is formally announced as sealed because of an emergency, and passengers are repeatedly instructed to cooperate with the Royal Army and avoid suspicious behavior.

Tserriednich remains in Zetsu and immediately notices that the casino is strangely devoid of soldiers despite the emergency. The chapter remains on **Voyage Day 12** and is linear in presentation. No exact clock time is supplied.

## Casino casualty trail

Tserriednich finds Royal Army corpses throughout the casino and adjoining route. The wounds are consistent: throats slit, with no evidence of gunfire in the killings he inspects.

He first considers whether a casino guest or one of the mafia families could be responsible. Later, after encountering Hisoka and observing the same throat-cutting method in his predicted future, Tserriednich becomes confident that the continuous corpse trail was caused by the man he met.

The maintained archive must preserve the epistemic distinction: the soldiers are directly observed dead, while **Hisoka's responsibility for the full trail is Tserriednich's strong inference rather than an omnisciently witnessed sequence**.

## Hisoka encounter

Tserriednich hears a machine playing in the otherwise emptied casino and sees **Hisoka Morow** seated at a gambling machine. He initially interprets the behavior as absurdly casual under Special Martial Law, then considers the possibility that Hisoka killed the soldiers and simply resumed gambling.

As Tserriednich approaches the approximately 36-meter operating radius he established in Chapter 419, he maintains Zetsu and plans to fire a test shot before activating his future ability to measure Hisoka's reaction.

When Tserriednich peers around cover, Hisoka has vanished from the machine and is suddenly above/behind his position on the slot machines. Tserriednich reflexively closes his eyes and activates the future ability.

## Predicted death versus actual movement

The future sequence shows Hisoka asking Tserriednich who he is. Tserriednich answers by firing. Hisoka then slits his throat, discovers that he is not a **Spider**, and reacts with disappointment.

This is a predicted/perceived future outcome, **not Tserriednich's actual death**.

When ordinary awareness resumes, Tserriednich hears the question again and immediately jumps away. He survives and watches the predicted scene begin playing out in front of him while Hisoka behaves as though he has just killed the version of Tserriednich presented by the ability.

Do not collapse the predicted throat-slitting and Tserriednich's actual escape into one physical sequence.

## Hisoka anomaly-detection ambiguity

After the predicted kill, Hisoka appears to sense that something is wrong. He looks toward the position where Tserriednich expects his Guardian Spirit Beast to be, then later looks directly toward the real Tserriednich with a glow/aura emphasis in his eyes before simply walking away.

Tserriednich interprets this as extraordinary Nen perception and is alarmed by the possibility that Hisoka noticed the Nen Beast and/or the hidden real him.

The exact mechanic is **not resolved** by the supplied synopsis. Do not promote any of the following to confirmed fact:

- that Hisoka definitely saw the Guardian Spirit Beast;
- that Hisoka definitely saw the real Tserriednich through Parallel Future;
- that Gyo or any specifically named technique was used;
- that Hisoka fully understood Tserriednich's ability;
- that Hisoka deliberately spared Tserriednich after complete identification.

The maintained state is narrower: **Hisoka detects an anomaly strongly enough to inspect the Beast's expected position and then look toward the real Tserriednich, but the exact sensory information available to him remains unknown**.

Earlier maintained material uses the stable archive entity **Parallel Future** and records **Ephemeral Ten Seconds: Laplace's Devil** as a supplied technique label in Chapter 419. Treat the Chapter 420 wording as a terminology/rendering variation attached to the same demonstrated ability context; do not create a duplicate ability entity solely from the naming difference.

Chapter 420 adds several operational findings:

- Tserriednich keeps Zetsu maintained continuously through the casino approach and Hisoka encounter;
- he uses the approximately 36-meter Chapter 419 estimate tactically when deciding when to approach and activate;
- the future ability lets him avoid the predicted throat-slitting outcome by moving away before the displayed sequence completes;

- by the exterior-deck endpoint, Tserriednich treats his remaining operating time conservatively and considers hiding while the ability recharges;
- he also considers dropping Zetsu as a strategic reset, but the chapter ends before revealing which branch he chooses or what the two resulting options are.

Do not assign a numeric remaining-duration value, a numeric recharge rate, or a fully specified aura-reserve formula from Chapter 420.

## Emergency-exit route

After Hisoka walks away, Tserriednich becomes exhilarated rather than retreating psychologically. He runs through corridors containing more throat-slit soldiers and heads for the emergency exit.

At the spiral-staircase emergency-exit room, he finds additional bodies killed in the same manner and becomes confident that the trail belongs to the casino man. He wonders whether Hisoka is trying to escape at all or is simply strong enough to kill Royal Army patrols in order to gamble undisturbed.

## Exterior Tier 1 endpoint

Tserriednich exits to the outside portion of Tier 1 and peers from cover around a pillar/building-like structure on the main deck. The large open area connecting Tier 1 toward the rest of the ship is packed with Royal Army soldiers, and the connecting bridges are gone.

This materially changes the practical escape problem but does not establish a completed route to Tier 2.

Tserriednich considers:

1. returning toward the Princes' Residential Quarters;
2. hiding somewhere on the Tier 1 structure until his ability recharges;
3. dropping Zetsu, which would open **two additional options** that still allow him to pursue what he wants.

The chapter ends before those two additional options or his final choice are disclosed.
