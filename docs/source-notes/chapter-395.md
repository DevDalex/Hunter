## Hinrigh’s camcorder evidence and the teleportation conclusion

Hinrigh considers the possibility of secret passageways but becomes confident that a Nen ability is enabling teleportation or equivalent spatial access to the Heil-Ly hideout.

The archive records this as Hinrigh’s strong evidence-based conclusion. Chapter 395 does **not** identify the responsible Nen user, formal ability name, trigger, range, directionality, valid surfaces, maximum links, duration, or complete hidden-room topology.

## Registered Heil-Ly office and joint pursuit plan

Hinrigh returns to Ken’i while all six of Tserriednich’s soldiers are present. The soldiers confirm that the military knows the registered Heil-Ly office on Tier 3, that the room was sealed after a warehouse-guard body was found there, and that Heil-Ly personnel were absent.

Ken’i says the probable secret hideout lies behind Room 3101. He says Nobunaga will investigate it with two other Phantom Troupe members, while Ken’i and Hinrigh will track the two people seen in the camcorder footage and try to bring them back alive so police involvement can be minimized.

Borksen says Tserriednich’s soldiers cannot directly help because Heil-Ly knows their faces. That preserves the identity-exposure and survival boundary established in Chapter 394. Chapter 395 does not give Borksen new detailed Nen knowledge or any later Heil-Ly recruitment state.

Ken’i says the Troupe was asked how it intended to handle the trap and replied that it did not matter. The archive records the Troupe’s willingness to accept the risk, not immunity to all traps.

## Room 3102 and the physical wall breach

Nobunaga, Phinks, and Feitan enter Room 3102, a first-class standard cabin adjacent to the Room 3101 trap.

Phinks argues that the hidden room may connect multiple standard cabins and that the ordinary door may not be the only trigger. He recommends beginning with the bathroom. Nobunaga then cuts a rectangular opening through the bathroom wall with his katana.

This is recorded as a **physical wall breach**. The archive does not label Nobunaga’s cut as a new Nen ability, a teleportation technique, or the solution to the hidden route’s Nen mechanics.

Feitan forces the room’s resident to enter the opening first, and the three Troupe members follow.

## Recently occupied hidden room

Inside the dim room, Feitan notes food and drinks and infers that people were there recently.

The archive preserves recent occupancy as scene-based evidence. Chapter 395 does not identify which Heil-Ly members occupied the room immediately beforehand, exactly when they left, or what route they used to escape.

The **Luini-as-scapegoat explanation is Nobunaga’s hypothesis**, not an archive-level confirmed fact.

This dialogue is preserved as the characters’ own comparison and as the narrative bridge into the Meteor City flashback. It is **not** promoted into a complete statement of the Phantom Troupe’s founding ideology, because Chapter 395 does not yet show the formal founding decision.

## Meteor City child-abduction and historical context

Chrollo reaches the All-Faiths Church and notices additional small graves. Father Lisores warns him that abductors have begun entering internal residential zones and taking children who never return.

The chapter’s narration explains that Meteor City residents had no recognized social status and effectively did not officially exist, leaving them vulnerable to exploitation. It says that before the city gained protection through its relationship with the Mafia, hundreds died each year and 70% of the casualties were children under fifteen.

The narration also says the city later received protection in exchange for providing Mafia recruits, that the elders eventually developed Nen abilities and established a law of retribution, and that the Phantom Troupe came into being as the world began to fear the city.

These statements are preserved as **narrated historical context**. They do not supply a precise date for the childhood scenes, a complete Mafia treaty, the elders’ individual Nen abilities, or the formal Troupe founding sequence.
