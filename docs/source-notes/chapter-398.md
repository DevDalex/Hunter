# Chapter 398 source note

- **Maintained story source:** user-supplied Hunterpedia Chapter 398 synopsis text provided in chat on August 10, 2026.
- **Reference URL:** `https://hunterxhunter.fandom.com/wiki/Chapter_398`.
- **Title policy:** no chapter title was supplied in the source text, so the archive does not invent one.
- **Chronology:** Chapter 398 returns from the Chapter 395–397 Meteor City flashback to the present Tier 3 Heil-Ly investigation on **Voyage Day 10**. No exact clock time is supplied.
- **No Chapter 399+ backfill:** later outcomes, identities, trap explanations, hideout discoveries, and later retrospective interpretations are excluded from this boundary.

## Front-door teleport trap

Chapter 398 directly demonstrates a prepared teleport trigger associated with the hidden-room front entrance:

1. The first unnamed Mafia hostage enters the main room through the bathroom-side bypass and **does not teleport**.
2. He walks out through the front door and still does not teleport.
3. He is forced to re-enter through the front doorway and **immediately disappears**.
4. A second unnamed Mafia hostage is forced through the tested entry and also disappears.
5. Hinrigh deliberately enters and disappears.
6. Nobunaga later follows and reaches the same concealed Heil-Ly interior.

This supports repeated/continuous activation at the tested entry. It does **not** prove unlimited use, exact switch geometry, total capacity, activation cost, reset rules, full destination topology, or a specific operator.

Gateaume’s displayed old-man double is **not visibly present** during these Chapter 398 activations. That observation weakens any requirement that his visible double be physically present at the doorway, but it does not by itself prove that Gateaume has no connection to the trap.

The archive therefore uses **“Heil-Ly Front-Door Teleport Trap”** only as a descriptive label. The official ability name, user, Nen category, setup procedure, exact trigger boundary, capacity, and complete range remain unresolved.

## Gateaume / Emission / Conjuration / Specialization boundary

Phinks and Nobunaga debate the trap’s mechanics. Their statements are preserved as character-level Nen analysis:

- Phinks initially proposes that Gateaume’s displayed double could act as a lookout/trap element and that entering a certain radius might force teleportation.
- Nobunaga argues that teleportation is associated with Emission and contrasts it with Conjuration’s suitability for creating complex rule-bound rooms/spaces.
- Nobunaga therefore proposes that Gateaume may be only the lookout while another ability supplies the teleport trap.
- Phinks notes that Specialization could also complicate that model.

Chapter 398 does **not** canonically classify Gateaume’s decoy-body phenomenon or the front-door teleport ability as Emission, Conjuration, Specialization, or any other category. It also does not confirm that Gateaume is merely a lookout or that the trap belongs to a different person.

## Barrier-type vs land-mine-type prepared Nen traps

The chapter provides general Nen-system exposition distinguishing two prepared trap models:

- **Barrier type:** uses Nen-imbued support objects such as ropes or paper talismans to establish multiple activation points across a wider area.
- **Land-mine type:** places an activation switch directly with Nen at a specific location. The chapter describes this as stronger and therefore limited to roughly **two or three locations**.

Those general descriptions are maintained as chapter exposition.

Their application to this particular Heil-Ly trap is more limited:

- Nobunaga observes no visible support objects and **infers** that the tested room is land-mine type.
- Phinks says stronger land-mine setups require the user to be closer to the entry or exit and **infers** that the user may be near the teleport destination, likely the hideout.

The archive does not upgrade either deduction into omniscient confirmation. “This trap is definitely land-mine type,” “the user is definitely near the destination,” and “the destination is definitely the main hideout because of that rule” remain unsupported as absolute claims at the Chapter 398 boundary.

## Biohazard transmitter-oyster expansion

Chapter 398 materially expands **Biohazard** through Hinrigh’s own explanation and demonstration:

- Hinrigh turns a physical transmitter into a **raw oyster**.
- He swallows the transformed transmitter himself before entering the teleport trap.
- He says the transformed object will return to its original form after its **aura is depleted**.
- He says transformed duration depends on **object size**.
- He estimates that this transmitter-oyster will remain transformed for roughly **two hours**.
- The paired receiver supplies **distance and rough direction**.
- As the receiver approaches the transmitter, the beep intervals become shorter and higher-pitched and the visual bar/light becomes redder.
- The receiver’s maximum radius is **one kilometer**.
- The receiver does **not** account for altitude; an unclear direction can therefore indicate that the transmitter is above or below.

These details are preserved exactly to the demonstrated/explained use. The archive does not derive a universal size-to-duration equation, exact aura-depletion rate, maximum transformed mass, simultaneous-transformation count, universal receiver function for every Biohazard animal, or rule that every transformed object can end only through aura exhaustion.

Earlier chapter snapshots remain bounded: Chapter 393 and Chapters 395–397 do not know the Chapter 398 transmitter-oyster, roughly two-hour estimate, size-duration statement, aura-depletion reversion statement, or one-kilometer receiver behavior.

## Receiver / ship-position boundary

Immediately after Hinrigh teleports, the receiver is interpreted as putting the transmitter roughly **500–1,000 meters** away. The Troupe also cites the Black Whale as approximately **1,500 meters long** and **800 meters high and wide** and discusses moving toward central Tier 3 to observe signal changes.

The archive records this as a search constraint and character-stated ship-scale reasoning. It does not convert the reading into an exact coordinate, deck, altitude, vector, or solved hideout position. The receiver itself explicitly does not account for altitude.

## Hinrigh and Nobunaga inside the hideout

Hinrigh reaches a concealed Heil-Ly interior after entering the tested teleport trigger. On arrival he finds:

- no person immediately in front of him,
- fresh blood on the floor,
- sounds from a nearby door,
- enough uncertainty that he remains combat-ready and reacts to a presence behind him.

The presence is Nobunaga, who deliberately follows through the trap. Nobunaga says he came to improve Hinrigh’s survival odds and jokes that they are playing co-op. He explains that Phinks and Feitan remained more skeptical of Hinrigh’s plan. Hinrigh and Nobunaga then choose to move together.

This establishes a **temporary tactical partnership**, not permanent friendship, Mafia/Troupe merger, Xi-Yu membership for Nobunaga, or Phantom Troupe membership for Hinrigh.

## Self-restoring Nen-protected wall/stage

Nobunaga strikes a hideout wall repeatedly with his katana. The resulting damage rapidly disappears. Hinrigh recognizes that the space is protected by Nen.

The archive treats the self-restoring damage as directly observed. It uses **“Heil-Ly Self-Restoring Hideout Stage”** only as a descriptive label for that observed Nen-protected phenomenon.

Nobunaga then supplies analysis:

- Conjuration, Transmutation, or Specialization could be involved.
- Prepared Nen stages can use contracts in which the stage is stronger when the user is closer.
- Because his katana cannot cut the wall, he guesses the user is inside.

Those points remain Nobunaga’s analysis. Chapter 398 does not confirm the category, exact contract, user identity, user distance, protected footprint, durability ceiling, restoration limit, aura cost, or duration.

Morena is shown smiling elsewhere during this sequence. That juxtaposition does **not** prove Morena personally operates the self-restoring wall, the front-door teleport trigger, or every Nen component of the Heil-Ly hideout.

## Hideout topology boundary

Hinrigh opens doors while Nobunaga watches for a disappearance trigger. They directly observe:

- a shower room,
- a bathroom,
- three toilets,
- and, at the chapter endpoint, a room filled with laundry.

These observations enlarge the known local interior but do not establish a complete floor plan, total hideout size, occupant capacity, exact ship coordinates, complete route graph, or the relation of every Chapter 394 disposal/laundry/processing route to the rooms seen in Chapter 398.

The Chapter 398 endpoint is therefore **Hinrigh and Nobunaga looking into the laundry-filled room**. Anything beyond that room belongs to Chapter 399 or later unless separately established by earlier material.
