## Parallel Future / Zetsu boundary

Confirmed from Tserriednich's direct experiment or established self-observation in the supplied synopsis:

- activation requires Tserriednich to be in **Zetsu** with his eyes shut;
- he experiences a vision of the next ten seconds while essentially no ordinary time passes for observers;
- after the vision, opening his eyes allows the witnessed ten seconds to begin playing out in reality;
- during that future, Tserriednich can change his own location or physical state but cannot directly change another person's location or physical state;
- he can alter objects, but an altered object cannot be used to make an affected person perceive an interaction outside the future they are following;
- remaining in Zetsu after the original ten-second playback can keep affected observers perceiving the predicted Tserriednich/world-state rather than his actual actions;
- the Tier 1 alarm does not break his Zetsu, while Tserriednich still believes pain can break it.

The following remain **Tserriednich hypotheses**, not omniscient universal mechanics:

- that the ability shows only consequences generated by his own action and therefore omitted the verbal water order from the vision;
- that an 'antenna' exists at the activation point;
- that the effect is spherical with an unknown radius;
- that increasing static reflects weakening reception with distance from the activation point;
- that leaving the area of effect would instantly collapse the future/continued perception back into ordinary reality;
- that observers outside the area would be unaffected and would see the same reality Tserriednich sees;
- the exact aura-storage/battery formula beyond Tserriednich's estimate that usable duration is about one-eleventh of accumulated charge time.

## Chapter 417 reality question resolved

Chapter 417 correctly preserved the battered body and wider Room 1004 scene as unresolved because Salkov did not know what he was seeing.

Chapter 418 resolves the reader-facing question: **Tserriednich is alive and mobile during Benjamin's assault while affected observers continue perceiving the predicted/staged version of events.** Benjamin's shooting and stomping remain real actions from his own perspective and from the perception imposed on affected observers, but they do not establish actual fatal trauma to the concealed Tserriednich.

Salkov's doubts were therefore directionally correct, although Chapter 418 does not establish that Salkov fully understands the ability.

## Staged death and coffin plan

Tserriednich deliberately turns the continued perception effect into an escape window:

- he orders his guards not to resist Benjamin's forces;
- he asks Salkov for his gun and tells him to report exactly what he sees;
- he allows the room to proceed as though Benjamin killed him;
- he writes a last will banning viewings and funeral formalities;
- he uses surrendered guns as weight/filler for the coffin;
- he changes clothes and plans to leave before the stored aura/effect is exhausted;
- he considers his father's handling of the coffin as a way to delay discovery;
- he recognizes that guards or soldiers who know he should be dead are identification risks.

The coffin/body illusion remains convincing enough for Salkov to question how weight, blood, smell, and gore can be perceived. The synopsis does not establish the deeper mechanism producing every sensory detail, so do not over-explain it.

## Escape-route boundary

Tserriednich identifies three Tier 1 routes in his planning:

- **Route A**: starboard route reserved for royalty;
- **Route B**: stern route associated with Mafia and soldiers;
- **Route C**: general-use route connected to Tier 1's port side.

He predicts Benjamin will likely use Route C toward the Justice Bureau and chooses **Route A** as his preferred escape route. This is planning, not a confirmed successful traversal.
